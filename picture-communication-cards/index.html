How to use them

  1. Print the pages, ideally on card, and cut out the cards along the dotted lines. Laminating them makes them last far longer.
  2. Start with just one or two cards for things your child really wants — a favourite snack or activity works best.
  3. Encourage your child to hand you the card to ask for the item. Give it straight away and name it clearly.
  4. Build up slowly to more cards, then to putting cards together (for example "I want" + "biscuit").
  5. Keep the cards somewhere consistent — a ring binder, a board with self-adhesive hook-and-loop dots, or a small wallet.

A speech and language therapist can help you choose the right vocabulary and approach for your child. These cards are a starting point, not a substitute for professional support.

Frequently asked questions

What are picture communication cards?

Small cards showing a symbol and a word. A child who finds speech difficult can hand over or point to a card to make a request, answer a question or share how they feel. They're widely used with autistic children and children with additional needs as part of AAC (augmentative and alternative communication).

Yes — the PDF is completely free to download, print and cut out. Because the symbols come from the openly licensed Mulberry Symbol Set, you're also free to share the sheet, as long as the credit printed on it stays intact.

What symbols do the cards use?

They use the Mulberry Symbol Set — a free, openly licensed collection of communication symbols designed for AAC. This means the cards are genuinely free to use and share, with no licence fees.

Can I make cards for words that aren't in this set?

Yes. The free online communication card maker lets you search thousands of pictograms, build a custom set with your own labels, sizes and colours, and print it.

How do I start using picture exchange with my child?

Begin with one or two highly motivating cards, such as a favourite snack or activity. Encourage your child to hand you the card to ask for it, then give it straight away and name it. Build up gradually to more cards and to combining them. A speech and language therapist can help tailor the approach to your child.
