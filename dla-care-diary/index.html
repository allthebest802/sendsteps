How to use this as evidence for a DLA claim

Disability Living Allowance for children is decided on how much extra care, attention and supervision your child needs compared with a child of the same age who doesn't have a disability or health condition. The claim form and any review ask you to describe a typical day — and it's easy to under-describe it, because when you do this care every day it just feels normal. A diary written at the time is far more convincing than trying to remember months later.

A few things that make a care diary genuinely useful:

  • Write it as it happens. A week or two of real, typical days carries more weight than a tidy summary. Include the ordinary days as well as the hard ones.
  • Record supervision, not just hands-on help. Watching a child to keep them safe — because they might run off, hurt themselves, or not recognise danger — counts, and it's the single most under-reported thing on DLA claims.
  • Compare to a child the same age. The test isn't "does my child need help" — it's "do they need substantially more help than other children their age". The diary prompts you to note this on every entry for exactly that reason.
  • Include the night. Getting up repeatedly, settling, checking, or dealing with disturbed sleep all count as night-time care. Log these too, or use the companion sleep diary below.
  • Say how long it takes. "Helped with dressing" says less than "20 minutes, every morning, prompting each step". Time and repetition are what show the level of need.

What the DLA care component looks at

The care component has three levels, based on how much help your child needs through the day and night. In broad terms it considers:

Daytime care and attention

Frequent help throughout the day with things like washing, dressing, eating, toileting, medication or communication — more than would be usual for their age.

Supervision to stay safe

Needing someone watching over them to prevent harm to themselves or others — for example a child who has no sense of danger, bolts, or becomes distressed and unsafe without warning.

Night-time needs

Repeated attention during the night, or someone needing to be awake to keep them safe. This is where a sleep diary alongside this care diary strengthens a claim.

This tool doesn't decide anything or send anything anywhere — it just helps you gather the everyday detail in one place so you can describe it clearly on the form.

Common questions

Do I need a diary for a DLA claim?

It isn't required, but it's one of the most useful things you can provide. A DLA claim is decided on how much extra care and supervision your child needs, and a diary written at the time shows that far more clearly than trying to summarise it from memory. It's especially helpful at review or if you appeal a decision.

How many days should I record?

A week or two of typical days is usually plenty. Aim to capture ordinary days as well as harder ones, so it reflects what care looks like most of the time rather than only the worst moments.

What's the difference between care and supervision?

Care is hands-on help — washing, dressing, feeding, giving medication and so on. Supervision is needing someone watching over your child to keep them (or others) safe, even if you're not doing something with your hands at that moment. Both count towards a DLA claim, and supervision is the one parents most often forget to mention.

Can I use this for Child Disability Payment in Scotland, or PIP at 16?

Yes. In Scotland, DLA for children has been replaced by Child Disability Payment, which looks at very similar care and mobility needs. When a young person turns 16 they usually move to Personal Independence Payment (or Adult Disability Payment in Scotland). The same kind of day-to-day record of needs is useful for all of them.

Is this an official DWP form? Is my information safe?

No — it's a free template to help you organise your own notes, not an official form, and Helpset isn't connected to the DWP or Social Security Scotland. Nothing you type is uploaded or sent anywhere. It's saved only in your own browser on your own device, and you can clear it any time with the "Clear all" button.
