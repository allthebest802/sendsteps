Free DLA Sleep Diary (Printable)

A simple, printable diary for recording your child's night-time care and wakings — designed to support a Disability Living Allowance (DLA) claim.

If you're claiming DLA for a child who wakes and needs care overnight, a sleep diary is one of the most useful pieces of evidence you can provide. This free sleep diary for DLA gives you a clear place to record what happens each night and — most importantly — the care and supervision you give. It works well for autistic children and children with a wide range of additional needs.

Why a sleep diary helps a DLA claim

The night-time care part of DLA looks at whether a child needs substantially more attention or watching over at night than a child of the same age without a disability. A one-off note that your child "sleeps badly" rarely captures that. A diary kept over a week or two shows the pattern: how often you're up, how long each time takes, and what your child needs from you to stay safe and settled.

What this diary records
  • Settling at bedtime and how long it takes
  • Each night waking — the time, how long, and the care you gave
  • Whether your child could be left safely, or needed supervision
  • Early waking and the effect on the following day
  • A weekly overview so patterns are easy to see at a glance

How to use it for your claim

  1. Print one daily sheet for each night — aim for at least one to two weeks.
  2. Write down what you actually did: getting up, resettling, changing, comforting, checking, supervising — and roughly how long each took.
  3. Record the time and number of wakings. Frequency and duration are what assessors focus on.
  4. Be specific. "Up 4 times, 20–40 minutes each, needed resettling and supervision to stay safe" is far stronger than "bad night".
  5. Attach the completed sheets to your claim, or summarise them on the weekly overview page.

This diary is evidence you gather yourself — it isn't a medical assessment or a guarantee of any award. It works best alongside letters from professionals such as your GP, paediatrician, health visitor or your child's school.

Frequently asked questions

What is a DLA sleep diary?

It's a night-by-night record of the care and supervision your child needs overnight. It helps show whether they need substantially more attention at night than a child of the same age without a disability — part of how the night-time care component of DLA is assessed.

How do I use a sleep diary for an autistic child's DLA claim?

Complete one daily sheet per night for at least one to two weeks. Record each waking, how long it lasted, and the specific care you gave — resettling, comforting, changing, or keeping your child safe. Focus on the care you provide, not only that your child woke, then attach the sheets to your claim.

Is it free to download?

Yes — the printable PDF is completely free. Please share it with other parents and with professionals supporting your claim.

How many nights should I record?

Aim for at least one to two weeks so a clear pattern is visible. How often you're up and how long the care takes matters more than any single night.
